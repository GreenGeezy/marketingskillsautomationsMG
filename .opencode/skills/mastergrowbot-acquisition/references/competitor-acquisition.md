# October18 owner correction: US iOS competitor acquisition

All content also follows references/content-quality.md: zero em dashes, no AI-slop patterns, a bottom-of-funnel keyword and value brief, sourced facts and a clear purchase path. A thin keyword-swapped page is a release failure.

Bottom-of-funnel is the default and takes priority over top-of-funnel traffic. Choose queries showing a purchase, subscription, download or switching decision. Do not create broad 'what is', general growing tips, awareness listicles or generic plant-health articles as a substitute. A specific price/platform/capability question qualifies only if the answer leads naturally to a supported app purchase decision. Optimize qualified store-click sessions and attributed paid subscriptions, not raw impressions alone.

Discover competitors before choosing topics. Refresh the private us-ios-competitor-census.json monthly or after a material market change using the repository's scripts/research-ios-competitors.mjs; use the current cached census otherwise. The free Apple Search API samples the US storefront. Confirm finalists in the US iPhone App Store and primary vendor documentation. Do not assume the sample is exhaustive or that prior GrowDoc/HiGrade suggestions outrank larger relevant competitors.

Classify direct grow-journal/diagnosis software competitors separately from adjacent general plant apps, hardware-only tools, dispensaries and unrelated games. Within direct competitors rank descending by verified US rating count, then buyer relevance and active availability. Record written-review count only when actually exposed; rating count is a popularity proxy, not reviews/downloads/search demand. Exclude unavailable apps and our own app. A large unrelated app does not become a good comparison merely through its rating count.

For the top five relevant competitors record: US listing URL/ID, date, rating count/stars, publisher, current features, platforms, price/subscription evidence and limitations. Check existing canonical pages before selecting an article. Existing bud, Jane and BudLabs pages already serve their comparison intent: do not recreate them or repeatedly rewrite them. Preserve them unless a real factual/technical defect exists. Choose the next highest-opportunity uncovered competitor, not a fixed preselected brand.

Evaluate one coherent buyer-intent cluster per competitor: '[brand] vs MasterGrowbot', '[brand] alternatives', 'apps like [brand]', and a specific supported software decision such as iPhone availability, pricing or journal-versus-photo-report workflow. Map these into ONE strong page where intent overlaps; do not make thin separate pages for every phrase. Broad brand-only terms are not assumed easy to win.

Before drafting, inspect US GSC query/page evidence and a small live search sample for the exact candidate queries. Save query, date/locale, observed results and their relevance, current ranking evidence where available, intent gaps, buyer fit and overlap decision. Assess relative competition from actual result quality/intent, not invented keyword difficulty or volume. Rating counts alone do not establish easy keywords. Prefer specific purchase/switching questions with weak or mismatched existing answers. If no query data exists, label an owner-authorized bounded acquisition experiment, keep demand/volume null, and explain the proxy; do not falsely claim proven demand.

Create evergreen titles and slugs by default, e.g. '[Brand] vs MasterGrowbot AI: iPhone Features, Pricing and Trade-offs'. No new 2026/2027 suffixes unless the year is essential to the question and maintained. Do not rename existing dated URLs just because the year changes. Preserve their traffic and canonical history; update genuinely stale facts in place after normal gates.

Write an original, useful software buying comparison: direct answer, who each app suits, fair sourced feature/price table, competitor strengths, MasterGrowbot limitations, publisher disclosure, dated sources, authentic assets when available, and genuine iOS CTA. Never invent hands-on use or claim universal superiority. Answer likely follow-ups clearly for AI retrieval without hidden instructions or special AEO markup. Keep content to product/software decisions, not cultivation instructions.

Prioritize the shortlist using buyer intent + rating-based popularity + observed query opportunity + feasible relative competition + distinct coverage, divided by effort. State the rationale in plain language. Publish only after release tests and production verification; then internal links, sitemap/URL queue, and 14/28-day measurement. A factual repair does not count as a new competitor-acquisition article. Keep technical repairs as a separate workstream and report actual new resources separately from edits.
